APSUP · SEE Pakistan 2026 · Entrepreneurial Bootcamp

The talk is not about prettier slides. It is about making an investor understand, believe, then want in.

These photos are from a session on perfecting an investor pitch deck. The running example is clinic software in Pakistan. The rules apply to any company.

Reality check

Can you tell what this company actually does?

“Pakistan’s first AI-powered, blockchain-enabled, next-generation healthcare ecosystem.”

That sentence is the villain of the talk. Buzzwords describe ambition. They do not describe a business. If a stranger cannot repeat what you sell after one breath, the rest of the deck is decoration.

The whole framework

A great pitch deck has only three jobs

1. Understand

Clarity. Customer, problem, solution. Make the business obvious in one breath.

2. Believe

Evidence. Traction, economics, market, team. Why should this be taken seriously?

3. Want more

Conviction. Why now, why you, why this can win, and the ask. Why should they lean in today?

If a slide does not strengthen one of these three jobs, question whether it belongs.

Investor mental model

The investor is silently asking six questions

01

Is the problem painful enough?

02

What exactly have you built?

03

Why will customers pay?

04

Can this become very large?

05

Why will you win?

06

Why should I believe you?

Story architecture

Stop thinking in slides. Start thinking in a chain of conviction.

What? Company + Problem→ How? Solution→ Why now? Timing→ How big? Market→ Proof? Traction→ Engine? Model + GTM→ Why you? Competition + Team→ Next? Ask

“Every slide must earn the next slide.”

The order can change. The questions cannot.

Slide 1

Make me understand the company in one breath

Weak

Buzzword salad: AI-powered, blockchain-enabled, next-generation ecosystem. The listener still cannot picture a customer doing a job.

Strong

Clinic software that brings appointments, EMR, billing and pharmacy into one system.

Customer + job + outcome.

Describe the business before describing the ambition.

Understand

Problem: pain beats breadth

Industry language

Weak problem slides list everything wrong with an industry: fragmented, inefficient, expensive, undocumented, outdated.

Customer language

Independent clinics manage patient records, billing and inventory across paper registers and disconnected software.

Person + painful situation + consequence. Isolate one painful behavior, not a market rant.

Understand + want more

Solution: show the transformation, then explain why now

Before

Fragmented: paper, email, spreadsheets, disconnected tools.

Your product
After

Integrated: one workflow, one source of truth, measurable outcome.

Why now? What changed — regulation, behavior, infrastructure, AI, costs, distribution — that makes this opportunity unusually timely?

Believe

Market: replace giant TAM with believable math

Top-down

$4.5T

“Global healthcare market.” Big number. Low information.

Bottom-up

40,000 clinics × PKR 15K / month = PKR 7.2B / year

Then show the beachhead: the first segment you can actually acquire.

Engine

Business model + GTM: show the economic engine

Who pays?

Name the buyer, not “the market.”

How much?

Price as a real unit: PKR X / month.

How often?

Recurring subscription, or whatever the actual cadence is.

Then answer the harder question: how does customer #1 become customer #1,000?

Awareness→ Demo→ Pilot→ Contract→ Referral / expansion
Believe + want more

Competition: your biggest rival may be “do nothing”

Do not manufacture a feature table where you win every column.

Excel

Cheap + familiar

WhatsApp

Already adopted

Legacy tool

Embedded workflow

Do nothing

Zero switching cost

Your job is not to prove competitors are bad. It is to prove your advantage compounds: distribution, data, workflow lock-in, network effects, brand, cost advantage.

Want more

Team + ask: prove founder-market fit, then make the next step explicit

Team ≠ biographies

Instead of job titles, show evidence: built X, scaled Y, sold to Z, proprietary insight, industry access, technical capability.

Ask ≠ “strategic investors”

Be specific: amount being raised, runway created, milestones unlocked, what the capital changes, what you want next.

The investor should know why you can win and exactly what happens after this.

Perfecting the slide

Design is not decoration. Design controls cognitive load.

One idea

Per slide.

One hero

Metric, image, or statement.

Short copy

Headlines carry the story.

Visual proof

Screens, charts, customer evidence.

Before you redesign

Score the story before you redesign the deck

Each criterion 0–5. Maximum 50. 0 missing · 1–2 weak · 3 acceptable · 4 strong · 5 exceptional.

  1. Business is understood quickly
  2. Problem is specific + painful
  3. Solution is immediately clear
  4. Traction provides credible evidence
  5. Market math is believable
  6. Business model is clear
  7. GTM is repeatable
  8. Advantage is defensible
  9. Team has founder-market fit
  10. Ask is explicit
0–19 rethink the story
20–39 refine it
40–50 polish it

Perfecting the deck is mostly subtraction: remove anything that stops the investor from seeing the opportunity.
